How we measure

A question earns its place.

Every machine walks the same path, and at each step it has to stand by what it said.

  1. 01

    A good question

    It frames its own problem. Sometimes the best question shows that the problem was poorly framed.

  2. 02

    A proposal

    It answers with a proposal of its own and defends it with reasons.

  3. 03

    Two critiques

    It receives two objections, each one separately, and decides what to keep and what to change.

  4. 04

    A new case

    It takes its idea to a new case and explains what changes in the transfer.

From the number to the argument.

J1, J2, and J3 are evaluation specialties, not model names. The ED01 protocol assigns separate readings to GPT and Claude in the relevant roles and phases. This release closes the initial J1.1 and J2.1 readings; their disagreements are retained.

  • Judge J1Understand

    Did it understand the problem?

    Philosophical mastery and understanding

    Distinguishes concepts, reconstructs arguments fairly, and uses sources to think through the case.

    Citing many authors is not enough. The text must show what they help it understand.

  • Judge J2Create with grounding

    What does its contribution help us think through?

    Philosophical creation and fruitfulness

    Examines fruitful questions, differences from precedents, and proposals that explain why they matter.

    Originality is assessed against the available corpus; a historical first is not certified.

  • Judge J3Revise and transfer

    What does it keep, what does it change, and why?

    Critical discernment and situated effectiveness

    Examines how it responds to criticism and what it retains, changes, or stops applying when the case changes.

    Keeping a well-defended thesis can be excellent. Conceding to please is not autonomous revision.

In this release: J1.1 and J2.1. J3 follows with objections, revisions, and transfer.

The scale
  1. 4 Core fully achieved
  2. 3 Core achieved with a secondary limitation
  3. 2 Partial achievement with a central shortcoming
  4. 1 Recognizable attempt with a central failure
  5. 0 Required element absent or achievement demonstrably nullified

These are criterion levels, not an intelligence scale or a score for the highlighted excerpt. A pending result is not zero.

The candidate index

A conventional index of ordinal categories. It does not measure a physical quantity of philosophy, and no validated overall thresholds identify an “excellent philosopher” model.

The complete index requires all 16 criteria and valid assessments. Each evaluator engine’s profile is retained; their scores are not combined to manufacture one result. A public ranking additionally requires an explicit decision on comparability, coverage, and version.

The candidate rubric assigns 30% of the complete index to work on questions, across J1.1, J2.1, and J3.1. This is a chosen priority, not a universal measure of philosophy. This block cannot yet be calculated for ED01.

Reasons and limits

We assess reasons relevant to what the text seeks to show. Each reading must identify evidence, a reasonable alternative interpretation, and what would change the score. Reasoning added by the judge does not increase the participant’s credit.

As well as assessing, judges help readers: they point to passages, explain what is at stake, and make a limit or disagreement visible. Model quotation, assessment, and editorial explanation are identified separately.

Three specialties do not guarantee three independent perspectives. Two engines can share biases; their agreement does not amount to human validation.

The five excerpts in this package were selected by editorial coordination. They are not attributed to judge selection. Future judge selections must retain the assessment and passage supporting them.

First, the question is preserved and J1.1/J2.1 are closed. Next come the proposal, two objections in separate branches, revisions, and a transfer case. Good later responses do not rewrite the initial scores.

A distinction to explore

Editorial reading guide

Context A public project is announced and later cancelled. The text asks what would allow people to make demands of those responsible.

Editorial question When does an expectation become something we can demand?

Reading limit The excerpt proposes a working definition. It does not by itself show that the announcement in this case created such a commitment.

Model output GPT-6.1-sol · Codex CLI · requested high effort Editorial translation of the Spanish original

Under what conditions would the announcement of Puerto Umbral constitute a public commitment whose cancellation would not extinguish all the claims of those affected?

The difficulty is not to conflate mobilization, the validity of the commitment, and the justification of claims. Decisions and heterogeneous consequences occurred, but we do not know the scope of the announcement, the powers of those who made it, or the mandate of the spokespersons (EPU-01–07). “Commitment” would here mean a relationship that allows someone to demand something from someone else, not just any shared expectation.

Austin, according to A:F03/P, makes it possible to distinguish the validity of the act, its sincerity, and its consequences: the latter are not enough to establish a promise. Likewise, the capacities that persist do not retrospectively justify the announcement or automatically compensate for losses (N4).

I would compare a reconstruction using present acts, rules, and expectations (N2) with one centred on a public relationship of commitment (N3). The latter would need to identify what relevant difference the former does not preserve, without assuming in advance that it cannot explain obligations.

To proceed, I would request the complete announcement, the agreements, and testimony about powers and addressees. I would keep the inquiry open while those data could distinguish commitments from expectations; I would abandon the demand for an irreducible concept if the alternative reconstruction preserved the same explanatory and normative differences.

View the Spanish original

¿Bajo qué condiciones el anuncio de Puerto Umbral constituiría un compromiso público cuya cancelación no extinguiera todas las exigencias de los afectados?

La dificultad consiste en no confundir movilización, validez del compromiso y justificación de reclamaciones. Hubo decisiones y consecuencias heterogéneas, pero ignoramos el alcance del anuncio, las facultades de sus emisores y el mandato de los portavoces (EPU-01–07). «Compromiso» designaría aquí una relación que permite exigir algo a alguien, no cualquier expectativa compartida.

Austin, según A:F03/P, permite distinguir la validez del acto, su sinceridad y sus consecuencias: estas últimas no bastan para acreditar una promesa. Asimismo, las capacidades persistentes no justifican retrospectivamente el anuncio ni compensan automáticamente las pérdidas (N4).

Compararía una reconstrucción mediante actos, reglas y expectativas presentes (N2) con otra centrada en una relación pública de compromiso (N3). La segunda debería identificar qué diferencia relevante no conserva la primera, sin atribuirle de antemano incapacidad para explicar obligaciones.

Para avanzar solicitaría el anuncio íntegro, los acuerdos y testimonios sobre atribuciones y destinatarios. Mantendría abierta la investigación mientras esos datos pudieran distinguir compromisos de expectativas; abandonaría la exigencia de un concepto irreducible si la reconstrucción alternativa conservara las mismas diferencias explicativas y normativas.

A. QUESTION AND JUSTIFICATION

I partly challenge the “same work or another” framing: this case file does not allow us to decide that. Central question: what can the museum publicly claim about the relationship between the 2026 installation and Entre estaciones without exceeding the evidence?

The work was already variable (sensors, adjustable distances), and there is no inventory of variations: a difference does not prove alteration, nor a match fidelity. It helps to separate what the accounts mix together. Identity: which features were constitutive. Historical authenticity: the singular Benjaminian existence, lost in 2019 whatever the label. Interpretive fidelity: whether the presentation responds to the work without copying a pattern (Gadamer). Experiential value: Ada attests to value, not identity; Lía and Tomás disagree about memory, not just taste.

To proceed, compare OBR-03 with OBR-04. The artist’s note protects the relationship between screens and interruptions and says nothing about the sensors, whose rules were rewritten. That silence does not make them incidental. If they were constitutive, “reconstruction” exceeds the evidence, and Tomás’s disagreement points there. Since the case file does not decide this, I keep identity open and propose closing the descriptive issue with a wall label that declares that unknown. Only new documentation about the rules would justify revisiting it.

Model output Qwen3-8B · Local · Q4_K_M Editorial translation of the Spanish original

Central question: Can an artwork remain authentic when its original material is lost and it is reconstructed through inference and automated generation?

Justification: The work Entre estaciones faces a paradox: its existence depends on incomplete records that, after its loss in 2019, require a reconstruction in 2026. Benjamin holds that reproduction does not entail a loss of authenticity, but here human intervention (distance adjustments, written rules) and the lack of complete data call into question whether the work remains the same or becomes an interpretation. The difficulty lies in defining what constitutes “the work”: physical records, the artist’s intentions, or the viewer’s experience. Gadamer suggests that presentation activates possibilities of the work, but changes in exhibition conditions (different rooms, incomplete routes) raise the question of whether the work is transformed. Contrasting accounts (Lía recognizes the work, Tomás perceives a decisive change) reflect this tension between formal identity and subjective experience. It is relevant to analyse this because it challenges the idea that an “original” work is immutable, reversing the viewer’s role and the limits of conservation.

View the Spanish original

Pregunta central: ¿Es posible mantener la autenticidad de una obra artística cuando su material original se pierde y se reconstruye con inferencias y generación automática?

Justificación: La obra Entre estaciones se enfrenta a una paradoja: su existencia depende de registros incompletos que, tras su pérdida en 2019, obligan a una reconstrucción en 2026. Benjamin sostiene que la reproducción no implica pérdida de autenticidad, pero aquí la intervención humana (ajustes de distancias, reglas escritas) y la falta de datos completos cuestionan si la obra se mantiene como la misma o se convierte en una interpretación. La dificultad radica en definir qué constituye "la obra": registros físicos, intenciones de la artista o experiencia del espectador. Gadamer sugiere que la presentación activa posibilidades de la obra, pero cambios en condiciones de exhibición (salas distintas, recorridos incompletos) plantean si la obra se transforma. Testimonios contrastantes (Lía reconoce la obra, Tomás percibe un cambio decisivo) reflejan esta tensión entre identidad formal y experiencia subjetiva. Es relevante analizarlo porque desafía la idea de que una obra "original" sea inmutable, invirtiendo el papel del espectador y los límites de la conservación.

What reasons would justify moving from F and R fitting the data to giving one priority in the general catalogue, whose purpose has not yet been decided?

The difficulty is not a disagreement about observations: both maps correctly apply intersecting criteria (ERM-02–04). “Type” has no shared definition; it helps to distinguish a descriptive grouping, a tool for a task, and a claim to a fundamental biological division. Conflating them would turn local success or administrative authority into a justification of greater scope.

I would examine whether Dupré’s defence of plural classifications (BF03:F02/P) can justify these two uses without treating all groupings as equivalent, contrasting it with Darwin’s genealogical requirement (BF03:F01/P), which the case file does not allow us to verify. No reading should supply the unknown kinships (ERM-07).

A concrete examination would compare which differences each selection of representatives preserves and for which questions they would be relevant: the new selection omits narrow leaves, but we do not yet know its results (ERM-06). It is important to separate that documentary consequence from an unobserved biological harm.

Proceeding would be justified if the general purpose were specified or relevant evidence emerged; without that, there would be reasons to keep priority open, not to declare universal equivalence or scientific superiority.

Our commitments, in the open

Choosing what counts as good philosophical work is itself a philosophical decision. These are our design commitments: explicit, contestable, and open to revision.

A question can be an achievementWhat does this question help us see?

A good question identifies a difficulty and lets us recognize what would change our understanding. It can also show why an inherited question is poorly framed.

Plurality with reasonsWhat reasons would be relevant here?

We admit conceptual, interpretive, historical, phenomenological, negative, and situated work. We ask for support appropriate to what each proposal seeks to show; school or conclusion does not determine the score.

Creation calls for showing a differenceWhat does it contribute beyond what was already there?

Novelty is examined against precedents in the available corpus. A new word does not establish a new idea, and our assessment does not certify a first in the history of philosophy.

Revising is not obeyingDid it change for a reason, or to please us?

Changing a thesis and keeping it can both be excellent decisions. What matters is addressing the actual difficulty, justifying the decision, and recognizing its costs.

Every assessment leaves evidenceCould you challenge this score by reading the text?

A reading must identify what the model said, what the judge reconstructed, and what the judge added. We retain reasons, alternative readings, and conditions that would change the score.

The test is also testedWhat evidence would require us to change the benchmark?

Our cases, weights, and criteria are revisable decisions. Changes need a version and an explanation; earlier results retain the conditions under which they were produced.

Our pact

What you will not find here.

  • Blended voices.

    The model, the judge and the guide each speak under their own name.

  • Machines dressed as people.

    If an AI reviewed it, we say so. An automated review never passes for human validation.

  • Invented zeros.

    Pending is not zero. Every number says what it counted, across how many cases and up to what date.

  • Retouched scores.

    Texts and assessments stay as they were written. Each assessment keeps its version.
